Limitation of Liability & Indemnity

Version 2.1 · Effective 12 November 2026 · What changed

This page explains the limits on our responsibility, and when you agree to protect us. It is part of our Terms of Use.

In short

  • We do our best, but Knowtopia is offered "as is".
  • If something goes wrong because of us, our total responsibility is at most what you paid us in the last 12 months.
  • Your rights as a consumer under Indian law always stay.

1. Offered "as is"

As far as the law allows, we do not promise that Knowtopia will be error-free, always available, free of bugs, or right for your particular needs or exams.

2. What we are not responsible for

As far as the law allows, we are not responsible for:

  • exam marks, results, ranks or admissions, or decisions made relying on Knowtopia content (see the Disclaimer);
  • mistakes in content, including AI-read text;
  • problems caused by your device, browser, internet connection, or other apps;
  • loss caused by someone using your password because it was shared or not kept safe;
  • indirect losses, such as lost opportunities, that could not reasonably have been expected;
  • events outside our reasonable control, such as power or internet failures, failures of a service provider, natural disasters or government action.

3. Limit on the amount

As far as the law allows, our total responsibility to you for any claim is limited to the amount you paid us for Knowtopia in the 12 months before the problem happened (for free users: ₹1,000). For schools, the limit in the school's order applies.

4. Rights the law protects

Nothing here limits responsibility that cannot be limited under Indian law (for example for fraud, or for our breach of data protection law), or takes away your rights under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

5. Indemnity (your promise to us)

"Indemnity" means you agree to cover our reasonable losses, costs and legal fees from claims by others if they are caused by:

  • you breaking our Terms or Community Rules;
  • you breaking any law; or
  • content you share that breaks someone else's rights.

For a child, this promise is given by the parent or guardian who accepted the Terms. It does not apply to a child's ordinary mistakes made in good faith.
